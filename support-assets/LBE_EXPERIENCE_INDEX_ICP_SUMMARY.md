# LBE Experience Index: ICP and Operating Summary

## Purpose

The LBE Experience Index is Easol's living map of target accounts in location-based entertainment. Its purpose is to give the sales team a high-quality, prioritised list of experience businesses that:

1. Easol can genuinely serve with its current product.
2. Are commercially valuable enough to pursue.
3. Have a credible reason to consider changing platform.

It is not intended to be a directory of every entertainment business. Quality and actionability matter more than the total number of accounts.

## The ICP in one sentence

Easol's core LBE ICP is a modern, experience-led business selling pre-booked competitive socialising or timed immersive experiences, with a relatively simple inventory model, meaningful transaction value, and an opportunity to improve direct sales, packaging, customer ownership or operational control.

## What a strong target looks like

A strong target typically has several of these characteristics:

- The experience itself is the main reason customers visit.
- Customers book a fixed session, room, table, lane, bay or other resource online.
- The concept is designed for groups of friends, dates, parties or corporate events, rather than sports practice or facility access.
- The brand feels modern, differentiated and capable of expanding to more venues or markets.
- Food, drink, premium tiers, private hire, merchandise or other extras create upsell potential.
- The business has meaningful online transaction volume.
- Its current journey is fragmented, clunky or controlled by a third-party marketplace such as Fever.
- It would benefit from owning its customer data, brand experience and booking journey.
- The buying group is accessible enough for sales to make progress, particularly for the initial P1 list.

## Core segments

Every qualified account should be assigned one primary segment.

| Segment | Definition | Strong examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lane / bay / resource-based games | A group books a lane, bay, oche, table or similar resource for a fixed period to play a social game. | Flight Club, Electric Shuffle, Sixes Cricket, Darts Brussels |
| Private room games / experiences | A group books a private room or contained experience for a fixed period. | Karaoke concepts, Immersive Gamebox, selected game-show and escape-room formats |
| Timed immersive / narrative experiences | Customers book a fixed-capacity, timed theatrical, cinematic, role-play, game-show or interactive experience. F&B is attractive but not required. | Taste Film, The Murder Express, The Traitors Live Experience, The Cube, Secret City Adventures |

Current areas of particular interest include interactive darts, interactive bingo, innovative competitive-socialising formats and immersive experiences using marketplaces such as Fever or Universe.

## What is not core ICP

The following should normally be excluded from the active target list:

- Traditional bars, pubs or restaurants where the activity is incidental.
- Sports facilities, courts, rinks, gyms or simulators mainly used for lessons, training or individual practice.
- Classes and workshops such as cooking classes or paint-and-sip concepts.
- Traditional or "old-school" bowling businesses without a clear modernisation, growth or experiential story.
- Family entertainment centres focused on children's parties and walk-in arcade activity.
- Theme parks, attraction complexes, cinemas, museums and heritage attractions.
- Multi-tenant destinations rather than the individual experience operators within them.
- Businesses with no clear online bookable journey.
- Concepts whose operation depends on product capabilities Easol cannot currently support, unless they meet the strategic exception described below.

Escape rooms are not automatically excluded, but they are normally lower priority because values and upsell opportunities tend to be lower and Bookeo has a strong position in the category. Keep them in P3 unless there is warm access, meaningful multi-site value or a clear quick-displacement opportunity.

## Shape of supply

"Shape of supply" means the way the business's inventory must work for it to sell the experience. The question is not simply whether the business is exciting; it is whether Easol can model the way it takes bookings.

The cleanest fits are:

- One group books one resource for a fixed duration.
- One group books one private room for a fixed duration.
- Customers book one timed experience with a fixed capacity and simple ticketing.
- Multiple activities are sold separately, or one core activity has optional extras.

Important structural gaps to check are:

- Adult, child or concession ticket types that must share the same physical resource or capacity pool.
- Groups moving through sequential rooms or holes where start intervals are much shorter than the total experience duration.
- Groups that must be automatically split across staggered start times.
- Session duration changing automatically according to group size.
- Mandatory waivers or consent forms.
- Unreserved all-access passes using the same resources as reserved bookings.
- Packages containing multiple activities with different fixed start times.
- A proprietary app that is central to booking, membership or access.

Simple assigned seating is supported. A separate enquiry process for large groups or private events is also acceptable, provided the core public experience is bookable online.

## Qualification buckets

Qualification and sales priority answer different questions and must be recorded separately.

| Bucket | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Target | Easol can serve the business and no material shape-of-supply concern was found. |
| Target - verify | The business is probably serviceable, but one specific issue could not be confirmed from the public booking journey. The AE should verify it early. |
| Strategic Opportunity | The account has a known product gap or normally excluded operating model, but the commercial value is high enough that Easol might build for it. |
| Excluded | The business is not ICP, lacks an online booking journey, falls below the relevant commercial threshold or has a confirmed structural gap. Excluded accounts stay in a private log and do not appear on the AE-facing list. |

Use `Target - verify` only for genuine ambiguity. It should always state exactly what was observed, why it may matter and what the AE needs to confirm.

## Outbound priorities

| Priority | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| P1 - Quick Win | Strong fit, at least $20k estimated Easol CARR, and signs of a fast buying process, warm route or weak/fragmented incumbent solution. |
| P1 - Balanced | Strong fit with meaningful value and manageable implementation or stakeholder complexity. The preferred CARR range is $50k-$200k. |
| P2 - Enterprise / Strategic | Large or strategically important, but likely slower because of scale, procurement, franchises, legacy technology or product gaps. Maintain and count separately from P1. |
| P3 - Escape Room | Default for escape-room operators unless warm access or a strong quick-displacement signal justifies P1. |
| P4 - Below CARR Floor | A reviewed or warm exception below $20k CARR. Do not add new cold prospects in this category to the active AE list. |

The starter list should be weighted towards P1 Quick Win and P1 Balanced accounts so new salespeople can build momentum. Enterprise opportunities should remain visible, but should not crowd out more winnable accounts.

## Commercial thresholds

Easol uses a planning assumption of:

`Estimated Easol CARR = estimated annual TTV x 2.3%`

Current working thresholds are:

- Below $20k CARR: do not add as a new cold P1 account.
- $20k-$50k CARR: include as P1 only when speed, platform pain or warm access is unusually strong.
- $50k-$200k CARR: the preferred P1 sweet spot.
- Above $200k CARR: use P1 Balanced only when buying and implementation complexity are manageable; otherwise use P2.

At the 2.3% planning rate, the $20k CARR floor is approximately $870k annual TTV. The $50k-$200k CARR sweet spot is approximately $2.2m-$8.7m annual TTV.

For skip-type businesses with an eligible commercial override, the current Strategic Opportunity threshold is $350k estimated CARR. A business requiring mandatory waivers should normally be excluded; retain it only when waivers are the sole material gap and estimated Easol CARR is at least $1m.

## Signals that increase priority

- A warm relationship or known route into the account.
- Recent openings, fundraising, expansion plans or evidence of rapid growth.
- Multiple venues with a repeatable operating model.
- A founder-led or otherwise accessible buying group.
- A poor, fragmented or externally hosted booking journey.
- Reliance on Fever, Universe or another marketplace that limits customer ownership.
- Separate tools for website, booking, events, gift cards or customer communications.
- Strong private-event, corporate, package or add-on revenue potential.
- A clear opportunity to improve conversion, increase direct bookings or launch new revenue products.

## Important calibration rules

- Prioritise innovative, growth-oriented concepts over established traditional operators.
- Competitive socialising must be genuinely social. Golf simulators focused on coaching, memberships or practice are not the target. Include no more than two simulator-led businesses in a 25-account discovery batch unless there is a specific reason.
- Food and drink strengthen an opportunity but are not required for an immersive experience to qualify.
- Do not mistake a standard show, exhibition or class for an immersive experience. The strongest immersive concepts use interaction, narrative, theming, packages, sequencing or premium enhancements to create a fuller customer journey.
- Inclusion on Ismelka's Fever list is source provenance, not proof of ICP fit. Assess those businesses against the same rules, then flag qualified exact matches.
- Do not set an arbitrary ceiling on the total index. Retain every genuinely high-fit account, while keeping P1, P2 and P3 counts separate.

## Research standard

For each plausible target:

1. Use the creator's own website and live booking journey as the primary evidence.
2. Confirm that customers can select and buy a public product online. Enquiry-only businesses are excluded.
3. Follow the journey through product, venue, date and time selection far enough to understand the inventory model.
4. Confirm the ticketing partner from the booking URL or visible "powered by" text. Do not rely only on third-party firmographic data.
5. Record pricing, duration, slot frequency, capacity or resources, ticket types, group handling, packages, waivers and venue count.
6. Estimate annual TTV conservatively using reported revenue where available or capacity mathematics where it is not.
7. Record evidence links and keep uncertain assumptions explicit.

Use Airscale only for hard facts such as company name, website, countries, venue count, main contact and its existing CARR estimate. Ignore Airscale-generated descriptions and qualitative assessments.

## Minimum record for each qualified account

The internal Experience Index should retain:

- Company and website.
- Primary segment and qualification bucket.
- Outbound priority.
- Market and venue count.
- Business summary and reason to target.
- Suggested Easol sales angle.
- Estimated annual TTV and CARR, including source currency and USD.
- Current ticketing partner, booking URL checked and supporting evidence.
- Specific verification concern, where relevant.
- Source links and review notes.
- Warm-source provenance, including whether it appeared on Ismelka's list.
- Assigned AE and outreach status.

The AE-facing version should remain simpler: priority, company, segment, market, business snapshot, why the account matters, suggested Easol angle, TTV, CARR, assigned AE, outreach status and AE notes.

## Ownership expectation

The RevOps owner should treat the Index as a maintained commercial system, not a one-off research project. This means:

- Keeping the ICP rules and thresholds current.
- Adding and refreshing accounts from multiple sources.
- Running periodic quality checks on classifications and estimates.
- Capturing AE feedback and changing rules when repeated evidence shows a pattern.
- Keeping exclusions privately available for audit and reconsideration.
- Preventing duplicates and stale or defunct accounts.
- Maintaining fair account allocation while balancing priority, value, segment and warm relationships.
- Measuring whether P1, P2 and P3 accounts actually convert as expected, then using that evidence to improve the ICP.

## Agent decision sequence

An AI agent should process each company in this order and stop as soon as the answer is clear:

1. Is the experience itself the primary product? If no, exclude.
2. Does it fall into one of the three core segments? If no, apply the explicit strategic-exception rules or exclude.
3. Is there a clear online bookable public journey? If no, exclude.
4. Can Easol support the visible shape of supply? If clearly no, exclude or apply the strategic exception. If genuinely unclear, use Target - verify.
5. Is estimated CARR above the relevant floor? If no, exclude from the new cold P1 list.
6. Assign the qualification bucket and outbound priority independently.
7. Record the evidence, ticketing partner, TTV, CARR and recommended sales angle.
